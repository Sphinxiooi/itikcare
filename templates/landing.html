Under the hood, ItikCare trains a Random Forest Regression model on your farm's own daily logs to forecast egg yield one to three days ahead, and surfaces which factors — flock age, feed intake, temperature, humidity — most influenced each prediction. A rule-based prescriptive engine then turns those factors into concrete recommendations, and every recommendation is traceable back to the exact variable and rule that triggered it, so you always know why ItikCare is telling you what it's telling you.

Starting Duck Farming? Start Here

A few practical basics worth knowing before you bring your first ducks home.

{% icon "droplet" "w-5 h-5" %}

Water Access is Non-Negotiable

Ducks need constant access to water, not just for drinking — going without it measurably hurts their egg-laying performance.

{% icon "home" "w-5 h-5" %}

Orient and Floor Your Duck House Right

Build it running east–west to cut heat exposure, raise the floor on bamboo or slats so it drains and stays easy to clean, and cover it with rice hull — changed roughly every two weeks — to protect eggs from trampling and droppings.

{% icon "wheat" "w-5 h-5" %}

Match Your Farming System to Your Land and Labor

Extensive — ducks forage on harvested rice fields. Lowest feed cost, but more labor to manage and protect the flock.

Intensive — ducks are housed and fed on a fixed schedule. Tighter control over feeding and egg collection, at higher setup cost.

A mix of housing and foraging time. The common middle ground most small itik farms actually run.

{% icon "sparkle" "w-5 h-5" %}

Start with a Proven Egg-Laying Breed

Itik Pinas is bred specifically for egg output — a well-managed flock can lay 200+ eggs per duck per year.

{% icon "egg" "w-5 h-5" %}

Sort Eggs at Collection, Don't Just Gather Them

Separate large eggs — suitable for incubation into ducklings — from small or cracked ones, which are better sold as table eggs or processed into penoy.

{% icon "trending-up" "w-5 h-5" %}

Demand for Duck Eggs is Stable

Duck eggs feed directly into balut and salted-egg production, everyday staples in the Philippines — which is why itik is the country's second-biggest poultry enterprise after chicken.
